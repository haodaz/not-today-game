# objects-in-hand — requirements (author 2026-11-02)

The old things, held and used. Design source: `script/notes/物件手感.md` (Chinese, the author's decisions are marked). Rules already in force stay: each object outputs only what its channel allows (steering `voices.md`), the chick's screen is 32×16 integer pixels, dream nights double a screen's resolution without changing its drawn size, an upgraded object shows the smooth high-res channel and "helps" unasked (object-upgrade FR2), objects are held up at night to block the hand (night-hand).

## FR1. Two states: holding and using
- **Holding** (持有 ↔ 放下): clicking an object on 桌子 (or in the 道具 panel) lifts it: the room dims 40 %, the object rises to the centre, **upright, not tilted**, with a handheld breathing (1 px, 3 s). It is the GLB mesh with the front image projected (room-scene FR3), back faces from `public/objects/<id>_back.png` when present (Claude supplies), else the bright-average tint. Press-and-drag turns it ±60° yaw, ±30° pitch; release eases back in 400 ms. Keys do not respond while holding. Click anywhere else, Esc or right-click = put down.
- **Using** (使用 ↔ 退出): clicking the held object itself brings it 15 % closer; its keys light (hover) and respond; the screen runs. Click outside, Esc or right-click = 退出, back to holding; once more = put down.
- Keys are hotspots on the front image, as percentages of the object's box, from `content/objects.json` → `objects[].keys: [{id, l, t, w, h}]` (Claude measures). A press sinks the key cap 1 px for 80 ms, plays the object's key sound, and sends `key:<id>` to the object's screen program.
- The first lift of an object still asks the recognise question (recognise FR1); later lifts do not.
- Holding/using are session state. What a screen shows is derived from `(day, dream level, upgraded, key presses this session)`; nothing here writes world events except where a rule below says so.

## FR2. Screens
- Each object has a **program**: a pure reducer `(state, input) → state` and a `draw(state) → frame` at the object's channel resolution (held.ts already has the channels; add the missing ones: pager 12 seven-segment digits + 2 icons, calculator 8 seven-segment digits, dictionary 2 × 16 dot-matrix cells, tape none, card none, textbook pages, phone none). Programs live in `src/world/objects/<id>.ts`, tested as pure functions.
- Seven-segment and dot-matrix glyphs are drawn by code (no font files); the chick keeps its 32×16 frame set.
- Dream level n (0, 1, 2, 3 after dream nights 5, 11, 15) multiplies the channel by 2ⁿ; the drawn size stays, so the thing looks thinner/finer (the author: "你会觉得它瘦了").

## FR3. The objects
1. **Chick** (day 1): keys A (select), B (confirm), C (cancel). Idle: two frames alternating every 2 s, a turn every 15–40 s (seeded). A cycles eight icons across the top (meal, light, play, medicine, toilet, check-up, training, attention; the selected one inverted); B acts: meal → head-down two frames and a heart top-right; light → black screen with two eye pixels; check-up → `♥♥♥○ 7g`; others → a one-frame nod. C → idle. Sound `chick-beep` on every press, a half-tone higher on success. From day 3 it chirps on its own on the desk every 4–9 minutes (`chick-beep`, audible without holding). At night held up: the grave frame (existing). Upgraded: colour screen, auto-fed, always smiling. **It never dies; it is only taken away** (author). Back: the reset hole (day 12's post); pressing it flashes the screen once and does nothing.
2. **Textbook** (day 2): pages, drag to turn (`page-turn.mp3`, Claude supplies); the back cover's score table has one empty cell: clicking it writes 「96」 in pencil over 1.5 s, once per run (session flag; writes nothing to state).
3. **Pager** (day 4): keys read, up, down, fn. Idle dark. A new message (the day's number from content) → `pager-buzz`, the pager jumps 1 px on the desk, screen lit, envelope blinking. read → the number; up/down → history (10 kept); fn → time (game clock) and battery (one bar fewer per day from day 4, empty on day 9, still buzzing). Dream level 2 → the digits become dot-matrix (same string). Upgraded: translated Chinese sentences, auto-scrolling. Key sound: a high `key-press`.
4. **IC card** (day 7): no screen. Turn to see the magnetic strip and the pinch marks (one per use so far; from day 13 one extra that the player did not make). Pointer in the top third while using = held to the light: the card turns 60 % translucent and shows the coil and the line 「余额仅供参考」. Sound: thin plastic flip on turning (`plastic-flip.mp3`). Its uses (the door, the night) are elsewhere.
5. **Tape + walkman** (day 10): lid, play, stop, ff, rew, volume wheel. Lid open by default with the tape in. Play: the reels turn at true ratio (one grows as the other shrinks), the earphone cord sways; sound: hiss and reel noise (`tape-hiss.mp3`), then **the tape's content as a dialogue sequence** (author): each recording is a `say` sequence in `content/objects.json` → `tape.recordings[]` with `who` (a newsreader silhouette, `family`/mother, `child` for the player as a child) and optional `backdrop`; stop interrupts the sequence; ff/rew: a squeal and fast reels, the box shows nothing. Volume wheel drags the gain; at the top it squeals. Key sound: mechanical click (`tape-click.mp3`). Dream level 3: rewinding to the start and playing again adds one line that was not there before (content marks it `after: 15`). Upgraded: a transcript scrolls, the reels do not turn. After any ending the same walkman on the desk plays the theme (sound spec).
6. **Dictionary** (day 13): clamshell, closed when lifted; click to open (hinge loose: opens to 110° and wobbles once). Screen grey-green dot matrix 2 × 16. Full letter keyboard plus lookup, up, down, back, power. Letters type into row 1; lookup shows the Chinese gloss in row 2, one word at a time; unknown → 「未收录」. The word list is `content/dictionary.json` (Claude; 40 words, each with `gloss` and optional `extra` shown from day 15 as a second gloss line). Key sound `key-press`; power → a long beep. Upgraded: whole-sentence translation that also speaks (a TTS placeholder: a line in the box).
7. **Calculator** (day 16): 8-digit seven-segment, solar. Keys 0–9, + − × ÷ =, C, %, √. It lights only when held to the light (pointer in the top third while using); away from light the digits fade over 3 s and keys stop responding; the darker the room (ring), the smaller the lit zone (door ring: top sixth). Normal arithmetic. Turned past 150° pitch the display reads upside down: digits become letters per `content/calculator.json` (0→O 1→I 2→Z 3→E 4→h 5→S 6→g 7→L 8→B 9→G) and the table of ten strings the player can find (the first four are story numbers: 0.7734 hELLO, 345 ShE, 505 SOS, 555 SSS). No key sound. From day 17 the display sometimes shows a number the player did not press, drawn from those four. Upgraded: a calculator app with history, always lit.
8. **Phone** (day 18): flip open / close only; the power key does nothing. On closing, the cracked corner lights for one frame (day 18 only). Back: battery cover open, no battery, a SIM with one line of small text (content).

## FR5. Tests
- Each program as a pure reducer: snapshot frames for the chick's eight actions, the pager's read/history/battery per day, the dictionary's lookup and the day-15 extra line, the calculator's arithmetic and the upside-down table, the tape's reel ratio over time.

## Correction 2026-11-03 (author): using an object is a focus view, not "15 % closer"
Today 使用 only brings the held object 15 % closer; it still floats mid-stage, the chick's screen is the size of a fingernail, and the output is hard to read. Using must be a focus view:
- **The author's frame (same day): using an object is a camera distance, the same tier as 屏幕.** The camera levels are 房间 → 桌子 → (屏幕 | one object). Entering 使用 is a move like 桌子 → 屏幕 (the same walk-and-crossfade timing, 500 ms), the object's face becomes the whole stage the way the desktop does for 屏幕, the bottom edge shows the same 「退回」 hint, and Esc or 退回 goes back one level (to holding at 桌子). Holding (持有) stays as it is, an in-between at 桌子.
- **Focus**: entering 使用 moves the object to face the player, scaled so its screen area spans at least 34 % of the viewport width (chick, pager, calculator, dictionary) or, for objects without a screen (IC card, textbook, tape), so the object fills about 60 % of the viewport height; the stage behind dims to 70 % black and blurs (backdrop-filter 6 px). 退出 returns to holding.
- **Input**: keys are the hotspots on the front image as today; hover shows a short label (the key id, from strings `obj.key.<object>.<id>`, render nothing if missing); a press sinks the cap 1 px for 80 ms with its sound (as FR1). The dictionary and the calculator also accept the computer keyboard while in use (letters A–Z, Enter = lookup / =, Backspace = back / C, digits and + − * / for the calculator).
- **Output**: the screen canvas is drawn at an integer scale (nearest neighbour, no smoothing) so every pixel stays square and readable at the focus size.
Tests: in 使用 the screen's on-screen width is ≥ 34 % of the viewport for each screen object; the stage behind has the dim + blur class; keyboard input reaches the dictionary and calculator programs only while in use; the canvas has `image-rendering: pixelated` and an integer scale.

## Correction 2026-11-03 (Claude, after seeing it in the browser): the 34 % rule was wrong
The focus view must keep the whole object in view. Replaces the screen-width rule above: in 使用 the object's bounding box is at most 88 % of the stage in both dimensions, centred, with every key inside the stage; the screen canvas keeps its face-relative rect and only chooses an integer backing resolution (pixelated). The chick's screen is about 40 % of its shell's width, so a 34 % screen-width rule forced the shell taller than the stage.

## Correction 2026-11-03 (Claude, after headless screenshots of 838305b): the device screen must fill its glass
Seen in the game: the shells are right, but each screen canvas shows at its bare channel size (the chick 32 × 16 CSS px) in a corner of the glass, so the picture is stamp-sized. Cause: `glass()` sets the backing to the channel size and the CSS is `max-width:100%; height:auto`, which never grows it; at backing = channel the 1 px gap also cannot exist, and a dream-level frame (`finer`, 2ⁿ larger) overflows the backing.
- **Size**: the canvas fills the glass's content box (`width:100%; height:100%`), keeping the channel's aspect (letterbox inside the glass, centred). Backing = channel × 16 for every level (chick 512 × 256); the frame's pixel size is then 16 / 2ⁿ backing px (2 at dream level 3).
- **Gap**: each lit or ghost pixel is a square `scale − gap` with `gap = max(1, round(scale / 5))`; at scale 2 the gap is 1 (the dots look thin: the author's "你会觉得它瘦了").
- **Upgraded**: the `hi` canvas backing is the glass's CSS size × devicePixelRatio, smooth.
- **Small fixes in the same file**: keycap words come from strings (`obj.cap.pager.read`, `obj.cap.pager.fn`, `obj.cap.pager.light`, `obj.cap.dictionary.lookup`, `obj.cap.calculator.off`, `obj.cap.calculator.C`; symbols, digits and letters stay as drawn); the pager's side grid loses its ✷ (the 灯 key is the one backlight key); the dictionary gets the space bar from the draft (`data-key="space"`, a wide blank key under ZXCVBNM, the computer Space key too) and its program handles `space` as written in the next section.

### Dictionary space (author: "文曲星不能没有空格")
`space` appends one space unless the input is empty, already ends with a space, or an answer is showing; `lookup` trims first. Hover label `obj.key.dictionary.space`.

Tests: the device canvas's backing is channel × 16 and its CSS fills the glass; with a fake context, a level-0 chick frame paints squares of 13 (16 − 3) and a level-3 frame squares of 1; keycap text changes with the locale and the symbols do not; the pager has exactly one `data-key="light"`; the dictionary types "good day" with one space, a double space collapses, and "today " looks up today.
